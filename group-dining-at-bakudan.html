Group Dining at Bakudan: What to Know Before You Bring the Whole Crew

Bakudan works for group visits, but it helps to know how the menu and ordering actually accommodate a bigger table before you show up.

The Menu Has Range

With nine ramen bowls, a Not Ramen section (bibimbap, curry rice, bulgogi, salad), and ten starters, a group with different tastes — someone who wants heat, someone who doesn't eat meat, someone who'd rather have rice than noodles — can all order something they actually want without anyone settling.

Starters Are Built for Sharing

Ordering two or three starters for the table — Gyoza, Edamame, Chicken Karaage — while everyone's individual ramen bowls come out is a normal way groups eat here, not a workaround. See our full starters guide if you want the complete rundown before you go.

If You're Ordering Drinks Too

Stone Oak and La Cantera run full cocktail and wine programs, which tends to work well for a group that wants a real drink menu. Bandera's saké bar is a different kind of group experience if saké sangria pitchers are more what your group has in mind.

Call Ahead for Larger Groups

If you're bringing a larger group, it's worth calling your preferred location ahead of time to ask about accommodating your party size — that's the most reliable way to know what a specific location can do for a group your size on a given night.

Timing Matters More Than Anything Else

Since all three locations open at 11:00 AM, an earlier lunch or early dinner tends to be easier to seat as a group than peak dinner hours. If your group has flexibility on timing, that's the simplest way to make a group visit smoother.
