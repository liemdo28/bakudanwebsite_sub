Why Ramen Still Makes Sense in the San Antonio Heat

"Isn't ramen a winter food?" is a reasonable question in a city where summer temperatures regularly sit well above 90°F. But San Antonio has a lot of restaurants that do most of their real business during the hottest months, and ramen shops are no exception — the reputation doesn't match how people actually eat here.

Air Conditioning Changes the Equation

The idea that hot food doesn't belong in hot weather mostly assumes you're eating outdoors. Indoors, in air conditioning, a hot bowl of ramen reads differently — it's not competing with the heat outside the same way it would at an outdoor table.

Not Every Bowl Is Equally Heavy

If you want something that feels lighter even though it's still a hot bowl, Cilantro Lime Chicken is built around cilantro, lime, and togarashi on a shoyu and shio base — brighter and less rich than something like Garlic Tonkotsu. Shrimp Tempura ramen, with its chicken broth and shio base, is another option that doesn't sit as heavy as the thicker pork-based bowls.

Some People Just Want the Rich Bowl Anyway

Plenty of regulars order Garlic Tonkotsu or Spicy Umami Miso in July the same way they would in January — heat outside doesn't change what they're actually craving. There's no rule that says you have to order lighter just because it's summer.

The Real Adjustment Is Timing, Not the Order

If the heat is a factor at all, it's more about when you go than what you order — an early lunch or early dinner, inside, tends to be a more comfortable visit during peak summer than trying to eat outside in the middle of the day. Bakudan opens at 11:00 AM daily at all three locations, which gives you that option.
