Family-Friendly Ramen Night: Kids Ramen & Bringing Little Ones

Ramen doesn't always get thought of as a kid-friendly dinner option, but Bakudan has an actual Kids Ramen on the menu, and there's enough range in the rest of the menu to make a family visit work without much stress.

What's on the Kids Menu

Kids Ramen is a simplified bowl: chicken broth, chicken chashu, corn, and thin noodles, priced at $8.50. It's built to be mild and familiar rather than adventurous — no chili oil, no strong miso, nothing that's going to be a fight to get a picky eater through.

If They Want to Try a "Real" Bowl Instead

Some kids would rather order off the regular menu than get the kids' version, especially if they've had ramen before. Bakudan Chicken is the mildest option on the full menu — a shio (salt) base with chicken chashu and spinach, no chili, no strong miso funk. It's a reasonable next step up from Kids Ramen if your kid wants to feel like they ordered a "real" bowl.

Starters Are Useful for the Table

Edamame and Gyoza both tend to work well for kids and are easy to share while everyone's ramen is being prepared. Ordering one or two starters for the table alongside individual bowls is a normal way to eat here as a family, not just a workaround.

Timing a Family Visit

Bakudan opens at 11:00 AM daily, which gives you an early-dinner or late-lunch window before things get busier, if that matters for your family's schedule. Hours run Monday–Thursday 11:00 AM–8:30 PM, Friday–Saturday 11:00 AM–9:00 PM, and Sunday 11:00 AM–8:00 PM at all three locations.

One Thing to Know Before You Go

Stone Oak and La Cantera run a full cocktail and wine program alongside the food menu, while Bandera is built around a dedicated saké bar. None of that changes the food side of a family visit, but it's worth knowing which location you're headed to if the adults at the table are planning to order drinks too.
