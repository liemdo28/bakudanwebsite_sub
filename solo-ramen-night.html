Solo Ramen Night: Why Eating Alone at Bakudan Is Totally Normal

Ramen shops, more than most restaurants, are set up for solo dining — counter-style seating and a menu built around individual bowls rather than shareable entrées. Eating at Bakudan by yourself isn't a workaround; it's a normal way people eat here.

The Format Already Works Solo

Ramen is a personal bowl by default, not a dish meant to be split or plated for a table. Ordering one bowl and eating it isn't an awkward version of a group meal — it's the standard way ramen is eaten anywhere.

What to Order Alone

A full bowl plus a single starter — Katsu Bun at $6 is a reasonable solo add-on since it's priced and portioned for one person, unlike some of the larger shareable starters like the wings or Takoyaki.

Timing a Solo Visit

An off-peak time — right after opening at 11:00 AM, or a later weeknight dinner — tends to make a solo visit feel more relaxed than showing up during the busiest stretch of a weekend evening, if that's a consideration for you.

It Doesn't Have to Be Quick

There's no expectation that a solo visit means eating fast and leaving — take the time you want with your bowl. Ramen shops don't carry the same social pressure some other restaurant formats do around solo tables.
